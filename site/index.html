The problem

Coastal operators track red-tide risk across five fragmented sources. None are unified. None are site-specific. All are lagging.

Shellfish and finfish farms, desalination intakes, and beach-tourism authorities currently piece together HAB risk from NOAA bulletins, state agency PDFs, internal spreadsheets, their own grab-sample results, and eyeballed satellite imagery. Each source covers a different geography, updates on a different cadence, and speaks a different language. The result: decisions made on stale, incomplete information.

The solution

A unified, multi-site, continuous daily HAB risk-monitoring signal built from public data.

BloomCast fuses FWC Karenia brevis cell-count lab samples with NDBC shelf-buoy environmental readings (wind speed, direction, water temperature) to produce a single daily risk score for each monitored site. No more toggling between five tabs.

The moat

Why this is hard to replicate.

  • a
    Fragmented, ugly data integration
    State agency cell-count databases and federal buoy feeds are messy, inconsistent, and non-trivial to fuse into a coherent daily signal. We've done the unglamorous work of cleaning, aligning, and gap-filling across sources and geographies.
  • b
    Oceanographic interpretation
    Translating wind upwelling indices, temperature anomalies, and cell-count dynamics into operational risk requires domain knowledge that generalist data teams get wrong. The feature engineering encodes real coastal oceanography.
  • c
    Data flywheel
    Every operator's own grab-samples enrich the ground-truthed dataset. As the network grows, the model improves — and eventually unlocks the frontier: true multi-day bloom forecasting from clean-water conditions.
